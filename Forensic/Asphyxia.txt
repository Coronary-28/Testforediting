Which method of asphyxia shows MOST severe internal neck damage:

A) Complete hanging
B) Incomplete hanging
C) Throttling
D) Strangulation

Correct Answer: C) Throttling

Explanation: Throttling involves direct, forceful, repeated finger pressure, which causes extensive deep-muscle bruising and fractures of the hyoid and thyroid cartilage. Ordinary hanging usually causes little internal damage.

Eshrahly:
The amount of internal damage follows how the force is applied. In throttling the hand grips the neck directly. This explains the deep contusions and the cartilage and hyoid fractures. In ordinary hanging the body's own weight acts through a ligature, and internal damage is usually minimal. Massive damage in a hanging suggests a judicial long drop. The cause of death also differs between the forms.

Throttling causes death by:
- mechanical anoxia
- reflex cardiac inhibition
- cerebral anoxia
- delayed glottic oedema and choking

Hanging causes death mainly by cerebral ischaemia from carotid compression, with vagal cardiac inhibition and mechanical anoxia also contributing. Severe internal neck injury should therefore point first to throttling, or to a judicial hanging.

###

The findings on the neck: extensive laceration, tears in sternomastoid and fracture-dislocation of C2-C3 vertebrae. These findings are present in:

A) Suicidal hanging
B) Accidental hanging
C) Incomplete hanging
D) Judicial hanging
E) Autoerotic hanging

Correct Answer: D) Judicial hanging

Explanation: Extensive neck lacerations, torn sternomastoids and C2–C3 fracture-dislocation (hangman's fracture) with cord transection are the picture of judicial (long-drop) hanging. Ordinary hanging usually causes little internal damage.

Eshrahly:
Judicial (long-drop) hanging is the form in which the cause of death is transection of the spinal cord, and death is instant. The characteristic findings are extensive neck lacerations, torn sternomastoid muscles, and fracture-dislocation of C2–C3, the "hangman's fracture" of C2 (a bilateral pars-articularis fracture). This differs from the other forms of hanging. In suicidal, accidental, incomplete and autoerotic hanging, the neck usually shows only a ligature mark, and death is mainly from cerebral ischaemia, vagal inhibition or mechanical anoxia.

Autoerotic (sexual) asphyxia is usually by hanging, with anoxia induced deliberately to heighten arousal. It can resemble a suicidal hanging, but it does not produce this destructive neck injury.

###

Strangulation with ligature:

A) Transverse, complete rope marks
B) Associated with multiple lines on the neck
C) Marks are high in the neck
D) Same as hanging

Correct Answer: A) Transverse, complete rope marks

Explanation: Ligature strangulation produces transverse, complete marks low in the neck. This is the reverse of hanging, whose marks are high, oblique and incomplete.

Eshrahly:
Ligature strangulation is mechanical anoxic death caused by constriction of the neck by a ligature that occludes the air passages from outside. The constricting force is not the body's own suspended weight, which is the difference from hanging.

- mechanical anoxia
- reflex cardiac inhibition
- delayed oedema of the glottis and choking, which can kill even some time after the ligature has been removed

Other findings:
- abrasions and contusions taking the shape of the ligating material
- inward fracture of the hyoid, possibly of the thyroid cartilage
- contusions and lacerations of neck muscles

The manner is homicidal or accidental, but not suicidal. A person cannot maintain ligature pressure on their own neck after losing consciousness. Any ligature mark on the neck is presumed to be homicide (strangulation) until proven otherwise.
###

Abrasions found on the neck are always considered what until proved otherwise?

A) Hanging
B) Strangulation
C) Asphyxia
D) Drowning

Correct Answer: B) Strangulation

Explanation: Neck marks are presumed to be strangulation (homicide) until proven otherwise. This is the reverse default of hanging, which is more often self-inflicted.

Eshrahly:
Location matters in interpreting abrasions. An abrasion pattern on the neck raises suspicion for throttling (manual strangulation). Fingernails leave small crescent-shaped (semilunar) abrasions when pressed in, or linear scratches when dragged. These are classic on the neck in throttling. A ligature mark on the neck is a crush (pressure) abrasion that reproduces the shape of the ligating material, and it is presumed homicidal until proven otherwise.

Both throttling and ligature strangulation are, by definition or by default, homicidal. Throttling is always homicidal. Ligature strangulation is homicidal or accidental, never suicidal. Hanging, by contrast, can be suicidal, homicidal or accidental, and the manner is never assumed from the mechanism alone.

The dead body is lying on autopsy table. There is leathery, fine, tenacious, persistent froth at mouth and nostrils. The death is due to:

B) Drowning
C) Opium poisoning
D) Throttling
E) Traumatic Asphyxia

Correct Answer: B) Drowning

Explanation: Fine, tenacious, persistent froth at the mouth and nostrils is the sure external sign of drowning. It is formed by respiratory effort during submersion.

In drowning – one is indicated for time passed since death:

A) Washerwoman skin (maceration)
B) Froth at mouth/nostrils
C) Goose skin appearance
D) Coldness of the body

Correct Answer: A) Washerwoman skin (maceration)

Eshrahly:
Being in water changes the normal timeline of post-mortem changes. Cooling, hypostasis, adipocere and putrefaction are all altered by immersion.

- Decomposition in water is slow, because of exclusion of air, protection by clothes and early cooling.
- Hypostasis in drowning is often faint, because moving water keeps turning the body. A floating body usually lies face-down with the head dependent, so lividity appears in the face, upper chest and upper limbs.

For these reasons, time-of-death estimates in drowning cases have to be made cautiously.

C) Gagging
D) Drowning

###

NOT of the TRIAD features of asphyxia:

A) Congestion
B) Fluid in the left side of the heart
C) Cyanosis of face and chest
D) Petechial hemorrhages in skin of face and eyes

Correct Answer: B) Fluid in the left side of the heart

Explanation: The exam triad is congestion, cyanosis and petechial haemorrhages. Fluid in the left side of the heart is not a feature of asphyxia.

Eshrahly:
The classical features of asphyxia are found where air passages are obstructed by pressure on the neck or chest: congestion, oedema, cyanosis and petechial haemorrhages. Exam questions often use the triad of congestion, cyanosis and petechiae. Older texts describe a distended right heart and engorged great veins, not fluid in the left heart.

Points to remember:
- Increased fluidity of the blood was taught as a fifth classical feature but is now rejected, a classic exam trap.
- The signs are very non-specific and unreliable. Even several together do not prove mechanical asphyxia.
- If death occurs very rapidly, the signs may not have time to develop.
- The slower the death, the more prominent the signs.
- The correct approach combines history and circumstances, exclusion of other causes and cautious evaluation of the signs.
###

How are antemortem hangings diagnosed?

A) By the presence of cadaveric spasm
B) By vital reaction signs (hemorrhage in ligature mark, skin abrasion)
C) By livor mortis pattern
D) By scene markers only

Explanation: Ante-mortem hanging is proved by vital reaction in the ligature mark: bruising or haemorrhage in the base and margins of the groove, reddened edges, and inflammatory cells on histology.

Eshrahly:

###

Abrasions around the mouth indicate:

A) Smothering
B) Throttling
C) Hanging
D) Drowning

Correct Answer: A) Smothering

Explanation: Smothering involves a hand or soft object over the nose and mouth. Fingernail abrasions around the nose and mouth are classic.

Eshrahly:
Smothering is mechanical anoxic death caused by occlusion of the external respiratory openings by the hand or soft objects. Findings are multiple contusions and fingernail abrasions, contusions in the buccal mucosa, and ventral hypostasis in cot death. If the victim struggles, there may be bruises and abrasions on the face and lips and inside the mouth, where the lips are pressed against the teeth.

Smothering may leave almost no signs.
- Pressure over the nose and mouth only very rarely causes petechiae, significant cyanosis or congestion unless the victim struggles.
- It may be virtually impossible to diagnose in the old, the infirm or the very young. This matters for elder abuse and infant deaths that may be mistaken for natural or SIDS deaths.
- Plastic bag suffocation typically shows no classic asphyxial signs, and the face is usually pale. The "Exit" method is an example.

###

Histotoxic anoxia is caused by:

A) CO
B) Morphine
C) HCN (hydrogen cyanide)
D) Barbiturates

Correct Answer: C) HCN (hydrogen cyanide)

Explanation: Cyanide inhibits cytochrome oxidase, so tissues cannot use oxygen even though delivery is normal. This is histotoxic anoxia.

Eshrahly:
In histotoxic anoxia, oxygen is delivered normally but the cells cannot use it. The classic cause is cyanide, which inhibits cytochrome oxidase. The same mitochondrial enzyme is affected by carbon monoxide, discussed in the toxicology section.

How the other options relate:
- CO is classed as anaemic anoxia in the guide: it forms carboxyhaemoglobin, which reduces the blood's ability to carry oxygen.
- Morphine causes anoxic anoxia of the central type, by depressing the respiratory centre.

Lividity colour is cherry pink in CO poisoning, in cold, and in cyanide poisoning.

###

What type of asphyxia is caused by cyanide (HCN)?

A) Anoxic anoxia
B) Anemic anoxia
C) Stagnant anoxia
D) Histotoxic anoxia

Correct Answer: D) Histotoxic anoxia

Explanation: Cyanide inhibits cytochrome oxidase. Oxygen is delivered normally, but the tissues cannot retrieve it.

Eshrahly:
Histotoxic anoxia is a failure at the cellular level. Oxygen is in the environment, the airway is patent, the lungs and blood are normal and delivery is intact, but the cells cannot use the oxygen.

Contrasts with the other types:
- Anoxic: not enough oxygen reaches the blood.
- Anaemic: the blood cannot carry oxygen, as in CO poisoning.
- Stagnant: delivery fails, as in shock or heart failure.

Other points the guide gives about cyanide:
- It is listed among chemical asphyxiants, alongside carbon monoxide and hydrogen sulfide, where inhalation prevents utilisation of oxygen at the cellular level.
- Lividity is cherry pink.
- Rigor mortis develops early.
- It is not a cause of apparent death, because it causes rapid true death.

###

Vagus nerve shares the same root with:

A) Glossopharyngeal (CN IX)
B) Trochlear nerve
C) Facial nerve
D) Temporal nerve

Correct Answer: A) Glossopharyngeal (CN IX)

Explanation: CN IX and CN X arise together from the medulla and leave the skull through the jugular foramen.

Eshrahly:
This anatomy underlies reflex cardiac arrest in neck compression. Pressure on the carotid sinus sends afferent impulses along the glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX). The efferent limb is the vagus (CN X), which inhibits the heart. Vagal cardiac inhibition can therefore cause death without prolonged anoxia.
