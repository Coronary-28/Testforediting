Burking involves which combination of asphyxial mechanisms?
A) Throttling only
B) Traumatic asphyxia + smothering
C) Hanging + drowning
D) Gagging only
Correct Answer: B) Traumatic asphyxia + smothering
Explanation: Burking (the Burke and Hare method) combines simultaneous chest compression (traumatic asphyxia) with covering the mouth/nose (smothering), causing rapid death from combined asphyxial mechanisms without leaving obvious external marks.
###
What is the most common cause of inward (central) hyoid bone fracture?
A) Hanging
B) Throttling (manual strangulation)
C) Strangulation by ligature
D) Burking
Correct Answer: B) Throttling (manual strangulation)
Explanation: Throttling involves direct manual compression of the neck, characteristically producing an inward/central hyoid bone fracture, distinguishing it from hanging or ligature strangulation.
###
Which method of asphyxia shows the MOST severe internal neck damage?
A) Complete hanging
B) Incomplete hanging
C) Throttling
D) Ligature strangulation
Correct Answer: C) Throttling
Explanation: In throttling, the hands directly grip and compress the neck with intense, sustained pressure, causing tears in the sternomastoid muscle, hyoid fracture, thyroid cartilage fractures, and even cervical spine injury — more severe than the mechanisms in hanging or ligature strangulation.
###
The neck shows extensive laceration, tears in the sternomastoid, and fracture-dislocation of C2-C3 vertebrae. These findings are most consistent with:
A) Suicidal hanging
B) Accidental hanging
C) Incomplete hanging
D) Judicial hanging
E) Autoerotic hanging
Correct Answer: D) Judicial hanging
Explanation: Judicial (long-drop) hanging involves a measured drop generating massive kinetic energy, producing a hangman's fracture (C2-C3 fracture-dislocation) and sternomastoid tearing from cervical spinal cord disruption — findings not typically seen in the shorter drops of suicidal or accidental hanging.
###
What are the characteristic features of a ligature mark in strangulation?
A) Horizontal, low, complete
B) Horizontal, low, incomplete
C) Oblique, high, complete
D) Oblique, high, incomplete
Correct Answer: A) Horizontal, low, complete
Explanation: Ligature strangulation produces a horizontal (level), low (below the thyroid cartilage), and complete (fully encircling) mark, in contrast to hanging's characteristically oblique, high, and incomplete mark.
###
Which features characterize ligature marks in strangulation?
A) Transverse, complete rope marks
B) Associated with multiple lines on the neck at varying heights
C) Marks positioned high on the neck
D) Identical to hanging marks
Correct Answer: A) Transverse, complete rope marks
Explanation: Ligature strangulation produces transverse (horizontal), complete, low-neck marks, distinct from the oblique, high, often incomplete marks of hanging.
###
Abrasions found on the neck are always considered what until proven otherwise?
A) Hanging
B) Strangulation
C) Asphyxia
D) Drowning
Correct Answer: B) Strangulation
Explanation: Neck abrasions (from fingernails or a ligature) are presumed indicative of strangulation until proven otherwise — a key medicolegal principle guiding initial death investigation.
###
What are the characteristics of the frothy secretions seen in drowning?
A) Bloody
B) Turbid
C) White and fine
D) Coarse
E) Offensive-smelling
Correct Answer: C) White and fine
Explanation: Drowning froth is classically white, fine, persistent, leathery, and tenacious — not bloody or turbid unless there is additional injury.
###
In drowning, which finding is most indicative of the time passed since death (time in water)?
A) Washerwoman skin (skin maceration)
B) Froth at mouth/nostrils
C) Goose skin appearance
D) Coldness of the body
Correct Answer: A) Washerwoman skin (skin maceration)
Explanation: Washerwoman skin (macerated, wrinkled skin from prolonged water immersion) progresses in a time-dependent manner, making it the most reliable indicator of how long a body has been submerged.
###
What finding is characteristic of traumatic asphyxia?
A) Pale skin below the neck
B) A line of demarcation
C) Cherry red skin color
D) Adipocere formation
Correct Answer: B) Line of demarcation
Explanation: In traumatic asphyxia, chest compression prevents venous return from the head, producing cyanosis above the compression site and pallor below it, creating a sharp line of demarcation.
###
A clear line of demarcation on the body is always seen in which condition?
A) Hanged body
B) Traumatic asphyxia
C) Gagging
D) Drowning
Correct Answer: B) Traumatic asphyxia
Explanation: Traumatic (compressive) asphyxia produces a sharp line of demarcation between the congested, cyanotic upper body and the relatively normal-appearing lower body, a finding not characteristic of hanging, gagging, or drowning.
###
Petechial hemorrhages are typically seen in which condition?
A) Road traffic accidents
B) Riot crush asphyxia
C) Cardiac tamponade
D) Heat stroke
Correct Answer: B) Riot crush asphyxia
Explanation: Petechial hemorrhages (Tardieu spots) are a hallmark of asphyxia, especially traumatic/crush asphyxia, resulting from increased venous pressure rupturing capillaries in the face, conjunctivae, and skin above the compression site.
###
What are the mechanisms of death in mechanical asphyxia?
A) Only anoxia
B) Anoxia, pulmonary edema, vagal inhibition (reflex cardiac arrest), and cerebral ischemia
C) Only cardiac arrhythmia
D) Only cerebral hemorrhage
Correct Answer: B) Anoxia, pulmonary edema, vagal inhibition (reflex cardiac arrest), and cerebral ischemia
Explanation: Mechanical asphyxia causes death through multiple overlapping mechanisms: oxygen deprivation, pulmonary edema, vagal reflex cardiac arrest (especially relevant in hanging/strangulation), and cerebral hypoxia.
###
Mechanical asphyxia is a type of:
A) Anoxic anoxia
B) Anemic anoxia
C) Stagnant anoxia
D) Histotoxic anoxia
Correct Answer: A) Anoxic anoxia
Explanation: Mechanical asphyxia (hanging, drowning, smothering) prevents oxygen from entering the lungs, representing anoxic anoxia — failure of oxygen to reach the blood — rather than a defect in oxygen transport or cellular utilization.
###
Death in an environment with decreased atmospheric oxygen levels (e.g., high altitude, enclosed spaces) results in what type of anoxia?
A) Anoxic anoxia
B) Anemic anoxia
C) Mechanical anoxia
D) Histotoxic anoxia
Correct Answer: A) Anoxic anoxia
Explanation: Decreased inspired oxygen concentration directly causes anoxic (hypoxic) anoxia, since insufficient oxygen is available in the air to be taken up by the blood.
###
Which of the following is NOT part of the classic triad features of asphyxia?
A) Congestion
B) Fluid in the left side of the heart
C) Cyanosis of face and chest
D) Petechial hemorrhages in skin of face and eyes
Correct Answer: B) Fluid in the left side of the heart
Explanation: The asphyxia triad consists of cyanosis, congestion, and petechial hemorrhages (Tardieu spots); fluid accumulation in the left heart is not a recognized feature — right heart dilatation is more typically associated with asphyxial death.
###
How are antemortem hangings diagnosed?
A) By the presence of cadaveric spasm
B) By vital reaction signs (hemorrhage in ligature mark, skin abrasion)
C) By livor mortis pattern
D) By scene markers only
Correct Answer: B) By vital reaction signs (hemorrhage in ligature mark, skin abrasion)
Explanation: Antemortem hanging is confirmed by vital reactions in the ligature mark — hemorrhage, WBC infiltration, and early tissue repair — which are absent when the ligature is applied postmortem.
###
What is a sign of antemortem hanging?
A) Salivation/dripping of saliva
B) Tardieu spots only
C) No findings
D) Congestion alone
Correct Answer: A) Salivation/dripping of saliva
Explanation: Dripping or dribbling of saliva from the angle of the mouth toward the shoulder is a vital sign requiring intact salivary gland function, confirming the person was alive during the hanging (antemortem).
###
Hypostasis (livor mortis) in the lower limbs is a sign of which condition?
A) Complete hanging
B) Incomplete hanging
C) Strangulation
Correct Answer: A) Complete hanging
Explanation: In complete hanging, the body is fully suspended, causing blood to gravitate and pool in the lower limbs, producing lower-limb hypostasis.
###
Abrasions around the mouth indicate which mechanism of asphyxia?
A) Smothering
B) Throttling
C) Hanging
D) Drowning
Correct Answer: A) Smothering
Explanation: Perioral abrasions from hand or pillow pressure applied to obstruct the airway are characteristic of smothering, distinguishing it from neck-focused mechanisms like throttling or hanging.
###
What is the most common manner of death in choking?
A) Accidental
B) Homicidal
C) Suicidal
D) Undetermined
Correct Answer: A) Accidental
Explanation: Choking from foreign body airway obstruction (food, small objects) is most commonly accidental; homicidal choking (e.g., forced feeding) is rare.
###
Histotoxic anoxia is caused by which of the following?
A) Carbon monoxide (CO)
B) Morphine
C) Hydrogen cyanide (HCN)
D) Barbiturates
Correct Answer: C) Hydrogen cyanide (HCN)
Explanation: Cyanide inhibits cytochrome c oxidase (mitochondrial Complex IV), preventing cells from utilizing oxygen even when it is available in the blood — defining histotoxic (cellular) anoxia, unlike CO (which impairs oxygen transport) or CNS depressants.
###
Complete hanging (as opposed to incomplete) is defined by which factor?
A) Height of the victim
B) Weight of the victim
C) Gender of the victim
D) Degree of suspension
Correct Answer: D) Degree of suspension
Explanation: Complete versus incomplete hanging is distinguished by whether the body is fully suspended off the ground or partially supported/touching a surface, not by the victim's physical characteristics.
###
A 65-year-old found dead in seawater shows goose skin appearance, easily removable hair, and the right hand forcefully grasping grass weeds. This is most consistent with:
A) Mummification
B) Cadaveric spasm
C) Adipocere changes
D) Anoxemia
E) Usual postmortem stiffening
Correct Answer: B) Cadaveric spasm
Explanation: Forceful grasping of an object (weeds) at the instant of death represents cadaveric spasm — instantaneous muscle locking that proves the person was alive and gripping at the moment they entered the water, distinct from the gradual onset of ordinary rigor mortis.
###
Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) causes death via what type of anoxia?
A) Anoxic anoxia (respiratory muscle paralysis prevents air entry)
B) Anemic anoxia
C) Stagnant anoxia
D) Histotoxic anoxia
Correct Answer: A) Anoxic anoxia (respiratory muscle paralysis prevents air entry)
Explanation: GBS causes ascending paralysis leading to respiratory muscle failure, preventing adequate ventilation so oxygen cannot enter the lungs — anoxic anoxia, despite normal oxygen availability in the environment.
###
What type of anoxia is caused by cyanide (HCN) poisoning?
A) Anoxic anoxia
B) Anemic anoxia
C) Stagnant anoxia
D) Histotoxic anoxia
Correct Answer: D) Histotoxic anoxia
Explanation: HCN inhibits cytochrome c oxidase (mitochondrial Complex IV), blocking cellular oxygen utilization despite normal oxygen delivery — defining histotoxic (cellular) anoxia.
###
The vagus nerve shares the same anatomical root and exit point with which cranial nerve?
A) Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)
B) Trochlear nerve
C) Facial nerve
D) Temporal nerve
Correct Answer: A) Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)
Explanation: CN IX (glossopharyngeal) and CN X (vagus) both arise from the medulla oblongata and exit the skull together through the jugular foramen, making their close anatomical relationship medicolegally relevant to vagal reflex death in asphyxia
